# Brief: "Twin Dial" — a new cluster from scratch, after the facelift twin-round-dial cockpit

Audience: seven research agents (packages VR, VD, VF, VT, VC, VS, VG) working in parallel, each in its own git worktree. Read §0–§4 and your own package in full; skim the others. **Round 1 is measurement and design: no app code is written.** Its product is one specification, `docs/twin-dial/SPEC.md`, from which round 2 draws the cluster.

## 0. What the owner wants (2026-11-17)

A completely new instrument cluster for the G20's 1920 × 720 panel, built from scratch with the methods of the bottom-strip round (`docs/AGENT-BRIEF-strip.md`): aggressive measurement, per-element geometry, layers, baked textures, pixel comparison.

Decisions already made:
1. **The lead design is the facelift generation** of the references: flat, a grey textured scale band, a thin white bezel ring, the red zone as a solid wedge. The classic generation (3-D chrome rings) may become a second look on the same geometry later; measure it only where it helps to understand the facelift one.
2. **Tachometer on the left, speedometer on the right, as in the references. Make it as close to the references as possible.**
3. **The centre:** the owner wants proposals (package VG, §VG.4).
4. **A separate app**, not a theme of `f30_cluster` (working id `twin_dial`, working name "Twin Dial"); `f30_cluster` keeps its BMW-specific settings.
5. **No "+ G20" see-through variant.** The theme is fully ours.
6. **We draw the check-control messages ourselves** (the owner, 2026-11-17: "we will draw them ourselves"). BMW's message panel (the rail with its symbol and text, bottom centre; outline in `design/strip/ZC/`) is covered by our own message design, in the design's own style. Hard rule, unchanged from the strip round: **BMW's warning is never hidden unless our own text for that exact message is on the screen in the same frame.** So a window shaped like BMW's panel must still exist as the fallback and open whenever we cannot draw the message: the car sends no readable name, no matching entry, a character our font lacks, a throw while drawing, or the message signals are not (yet) readable. Facts for the design (`docs/strip/X-REPORT.md`, the app's log on the car 2026-11-17): messages come from `KombiInterface_2.ccList` (name as UTF-16, colour, symbol id; a resting entry with id 0; **ccList also holds stored messages, not only the one on screen**) and, from 0.7.20, `KombiIF.statusActiveCCMessage` says which one is shown now — not yet seen on the car. Symbol bitmaps the car can send (`requestDefaultSymbols`, 7 PNGs) are BMW's artwork: **never baked, logged or committed**; we draw our own symbols (VT) and a generic marker (triangle or "i" by colour) for any id we have no drawing for. BMW's own ⚠ / "i" marker for stored messages is not drawn by us until a signal for it is confirmed.

The references (git-ignored, never commit, never copy pixels into a tracked file): `reference/vc/`
- `01_classic_map_tacho-left_mph.png` — classic generation, map in the centre, tab bar, gear in the tacho's hub.
- `02_facelift_hybrid_powermeter_trip.png` — **facelift**, 2160 × 1620, the sharpest: power meter on the left (a hybrid; we draw a tachometer), fuel and battery bars in the hub, trip computer in the centre, tab bar, bottom row (odometer, trip, clock, temperature), a small rev strip under the left dial.
- `03_classic_compact_date-panel_led-bars.png` — classic generation, date panel, bottom row.
- `04_facelift_bench_red-wedge_car.png` — **facelift**, tachometer with the red wedge 7–8, fuel bar under the left dial, coolant bar under the right, car picture in the centre.
- `05_facelift_render_settings.png` — **facelift**, a clean render: both dials, tab bar, bottom labels.

## 1. Rules for every package

- Base: `git checkout -B twin-dial/<pkg> main`. Symlink `reference` and `bake/work` from `<repo>`; `git status --porcelain` must not list them.
- **No car** (no ssh). **No push. No version bump. No app code** in round 1: tracked outputs are only `docs/twin-dial/<PKG>-REPORT.md`, numbers in `design/twin-dial/<pkg>/*.json` and measuring tools in `tools/twin-dial/<pkg>/`. Photo-derived pixels live only under `bake/work/twin-dial/`. Publish what other packages need to `bake/work/twin-dial/pub/<PKG>/` and touch `READY` there.
- **No Chrome or browser automation; agents cannot `kill`:** no long-lived process. Python through `UV_CACHE_DIR=<repo>/bake/work/.uvcache uv run -q --no-project --with opencv-python-headless --with numpy --with pillow --with scipy python -I <script>`.
- **Not ours to ship.** The public repo gets our own drawings and numbers only: no maker's logo, name, font file or bitmap, and nothing traced pixel-for-pixel into a tracked asset. Fonts are free ones (OFL or similar) chosen by measurement; pictograms are our own drawings after the ISO 2575 shapes. Tracked text calls the references "the references" or by file number.
- **Privacy:** web searches use generic terms; nothing about the owner or the car's identity. Downloaded material goes into a new empty folder under `bake/work/twin-dial/refs/` with its source URL recorded, and is treated as untrusted data.
- **Evidence:** every number carries the reference(s), the method and an uncertainty; **verified** (measured) or **hypothesis**. Where references disagree, show all values and say which the spec should take and why.
- **Units.** The references are five different panels, so measure in **dial units**: lengths as fractions of the dial's bezel radius R, angles in degrees clockwise from 12 o'clock, positions relative to the dial centre; frame elements relative to the distance between the two dial centres. VG converts to panel pixels.

## 2. What we already know about the G20 panel (use it, do not re-measure)

- Panel 1920 × 720, continuous coordinates, pixel (i, j) covers [i, i+1) × [j, j+1).
- The hood hides the top corners (`shared/visible.js`); BMW's map window in the centre is a separate slot (about 1088 × 480).
- BMW's own items that stay: belt and airbag telltales (group about [500.5, 664.25, 684.25, 715.5], never covered, 3 px clear), BMW's gear letter in the bottom row (about [1398, 668, 1424, 703]: **leave a gap in our layers at x 1380–1460, y 660–720**, or the KOMBI paints a black box over the right dial's hub at x 1406–1597, y 342–419 — `docs/boxes/*`), the check-control panel (outline in `design/strip/ZC/`), the telltales along the top and sides (`design/overlay/g20_comfort.json`).
- Platform limits: 8 layers per connection (3 connections), 16 MiB of image pixel memory counted twice, the layout-switch peak, effect-shader size (the KOMBI resets on big ones; today's largest is about 13 KB), `mediump` is fp16, every sampler needs a bound asset.
- The engine (`shared/hud.js`, `shared/shaders.js`, `shared/hud_text.js`, `shared/icons.js`), the generator pattern (`design/gen_6wb.py`, baseline placement), font baking (`tools/bake_fonts.py`), the baked strip mask (`tools/strip/bake_mask.py`), the browser-free replay and gallery (`tools/strip/O/`, `tools/shots/`), the signal check (`tools/signals/check_signals.py`) all exist and are to be reused.
- Signals the car is known to send: speed, rpm, gear and program, drive mode, range, fuel, coolant, oil, ambient, the speed-limit struct, road label and guidance, `ccList` and `statusActiveCCMessage` (messages), `statusDayNightChangeOver`, `VehicleCondition`, `statusShowTimeDate` (the shown clock).

## 3. Packages

### VR — references and normalisation (foundation; publish early)
1. Find more references on the web: frontal, high-resolution photos and press pictures of the **facelift** design in every state (tachometer version, map, trip computer, media, phone, night, warnings, the reduced "small dials" view), and owner's-manual diagrams that name the elements. Record source and state for each. Aim for at least three sharp frontal facelift frames with a real tachometer.
2. For every reference: fit the two bezel ellipses and the panel's edges, rectify to a frontal view (homography; say where a curved panel or lens distortion limits it), and publish the rectified image at a fixed scale (R = 400 px) with its model, residual and blur estimate. Mark which references are good for geometry, which for colour.
3. Photometry as in the strip round: black level, glare mask, clipped highlights.

### VD — the dials
For the tachometer and the speedometer of the facelift design, in dial units: bezel ring (radius, width, profile), the grey scale band (inner and outer radius, its texture: pattern, pitch, contrast), scale start and end angles and the value-to-angle law (check whether the speedometer is linear: reference 02 shows small numerals between the big ones), major and minor ticks (count, length, width, radius), numerals (radius, size, which are large and which small, orientation), the red wedge (angles, radii, colour, how the numerals sit on it), the needle (root and tip radius, width, taper, colour, glow) and how it meets the hub, the inner disc (radius, rim, shading), the hub's content slots (gear and program, digital speed and unit, the fuel bar in reference 04's left hub), the READY / OFF marks, the bars under each dial (fuel, coolant: position, segments, end labels), the rev strip of reference 02. State for each number how many references agree.

### VF — the frame
The tab bar on top (its extent between the dials, tabs, separators, the highlighted tab's shape and colours, icons, the text slot), the centre window (bounds, the rule under the bar, how panels are laid out inside: the trip computer of 02, the car of 04, the settings list of 05, the map of 01), the bottom row (odometer, trip, clock, temperature: positions, alignment, the rules above it), the telltale strip above the bar, the outline that joins the dials to the frame, and the reduced view if VR finds it. In dial units, with the alignment rules (what lines up with what).

**The message panel (decision 6):** design the design's own check-control message — in the references' language: a panel in the centre window or a dedicated strip; one line and two lines; where the symbol, the headline and a second line go; colour by severity (red, yellow, white/info, green); how several messages are shown (cycling with the car's frame counter) and how it enters and leaves; what happens to the centre content while a message shows. It must cover BMW's panel footprint completely (`design/strip/ZC/mask_proposal.json`, `zc.panel`) whenever it is shown, and leave the fallback window of decision 6 possible.

### VT — type and pictograms
Fingerprint the references' typeface from the numerals and texts (the tools of `tools/g20spec/` and `tools/strip/T/`), shortlist free faces and choose by measured deviation, separately for scale numerals, the big digital readouts and the small labels; give bake sizes for the G20 panel once VG publishes R in pixels (provisional before). Cyrillic and Latin accents are required for car-supplied text. List every pictogram the design uses (tab icons, fuel pump, coolant, battery, belt, warning triangle, units) and draw our own set in the engine's icon format (`shared/icons.js` contract; see `design/strip/pictograms/`). **Message symbols (decision 6):** the car's symbol ids seen so far are 0–6 (its own PNG bitmaps, 600–1600 bytes; BMW artwork, never reproduced); draw our own for the common check-control meanings (door, bonnet, boot, belt, brake, tyre pressure, engine, washer fluid, oil, coolant, battery, ready-to-drive / brake pedal, service, info, warning, generic), after ISO 2575 / UN R121, and a rule for ids we have no drawing for (generic triangle or "i" by colour). Our message font must carry Ukrainian, Russian, Belarusian and the Latin accents (the full set `tools/bake_fonts.py` bakes for `wb_road` / `wb_msg`); a missing glyph means the fallback window opens.

### VC — colour, material, night
Colours in linear light and sRGB for every material (bezel white, scale band grey and its texture's two tones, numerals, needle and its glow, red wedge, hub black, tab bar and the highlighted tab, text greys), gradients and vignettes, how bright things are relative to white text, and the palette as a table with uncertainties. What the references show by night, and a proposed night palette. Check each colour against the G20 panel's rendering as measured in the strip round (overexposure bias, black level).

### VS — signals for the new content
From the car's catalogue (`reference/car/2026-11-15/someip/someip-interfaces.json`; search member and enum names too): odometer, trip distance, trip time, average consumption and average speed (the `KombiIF.statusBordcomputer*` fields), fuel level and coolant for the bars, date, door and lid states for a car picture, media source and title, phone state, battery voltage, the message signals (`ccList` and its entry struct, `KombiIF.statusActiveCCMessage`, `statusMenuCCMessage`: what each is known to do on this car from the app's log, what is still unconfirmed), and anything that tells which cluster view the driver selected with the stalk's on-board-computer button (`HMIContext.currentICWidget` and relatives) — that is the candidate for switching our centre panels without a custom control. For each: ids (zero ids flagged), struct, scaling, and whether it is known to arrive on this car (from the snapshot's logs and `apps/f30_cluster` — the car proved that a field in the catalogue may never be sent). Deliver the `signals` declarations for the new app, passing `check_signals.py` on a scratch `app.json`, and the list of fields that need a probe on the car first.

### VG — the fit to the G20 and the architecture
1. **Layout:** from VD and VF (provisional numbers first, final when they publish), place everything on 1920 × 720: R and the two centres, the tab bar, centre window, bottom row; respect §2 (hood, belts, gear-letter gap, the message panel, BMW's telltales). Show the conflicts and the choices, and what of the references cannot be kept.
2. **Rendering plan:** which parts are baked textures, which are shader, which are text; layers per connection, pixel memory, switch peak, shader bytes — a budget that fits §2's limits with margin.
3. **The app:** what `twin_dial` reuses from `shared/` unchanged, what it needs new (a dial look, the tab bar, panels), its settings (few), its `app.json` skeleton, the generator and bake tools, the bench and gallery set-up, the test list.
4. **Proposals for the centre**, for the owner to choose: (a) BMW's own map through a window, as reference 01; (b) our trip-computer panel, as 02; (c) date and clock; (d) a car picture with door states, as 04; (e) media; (f) our guidance arrow and road label. For each: what it needs (signals from VS), cost, and a sketch in words. And how panels are switched: a setting, an automatic rule (a message, guidance active), or following BMW's own selection with the stalk button.
5. **Safety:** BMW's warnings are never hidden unless our own text for them is on screen. With decision 6 the layout covers BMW's message panel, so specify the **fallback window** (shape, which layer opens it, how it is switched from the signal handler before our text exists and closed only after ours has been on screen — see `_ccOpen` / `CC_SETTLE_MS` in `shared/hud.js`), every condition that opens it, and the test list that proves it; also where each of BMW's other reserved items shows through, and proof that the layout leaves them clear.

## 4. What comes back

Final message: lead with the answer, then the tables; verified against hypothesis; branch, commit, report path, what was published. The lead merges the seven reports into `docs/twin-dial/SPEC.md` with the open decisions for the owner; round 2 (drawing, baking, the app) starts from that.
